# Anti-generic design review

The target is distinct, credible art direction, not simply more ornament.

## Common failure signals
- Identical hero structure across brands: small eyebrow + centered headline + pill CTA + image/card below.
- Purple/blue gradients by default; floating glass cards and gratuitous glow.
- Every section becomes a three-column card grid or alternating image/text row.
- Infinite badges, over-explained copy, decorative divider lines and meaningless metrics.
- Fashion-editorial serif paired with generic body font without deliberate typesetting.
- Random unsourced lifestyle imagery, multiple unrelated photographic treatments, repetitive stock poses.
- Empty beauty: striking top fold with no useful navigation, explanation or next action.
- Inaccessible text over images, microtype and links disguised as decoration.
- Fake testimonials, logos, awards, statistics, or service claims.

## Better alternatives
1. Choose one dominant compositional idea for the brand and let it inform page rhythm.
2. Vary scale, negative space and image crops with purpose, not random asymmetry.
3. Use factual, concise content specific to the concept. Make imagery communicate context.
4. Take advantage of signature industry details: class schedule for Pilates, project index for architecture, provenance for craft, etc.
5. Establish hierarchy by size, structure and contrast before adding animation.
6. Judge against existing work: if swapping the logo and palette makes two brands interchangeable, rethink the concept.

## Non-mechanical assessment
During visual QA, score each 1–5 with **specific screenshot evidence**:
- Identity distinction
- Imagery coherence
- Typographic character
- Editorial composition and pacing
- UX clarity
- Responsive craft

A score isn't a benchmark guarantee. Explain weaknesses and prioritize fixes. Do not claim Awwwards quality or automatic design excellence.
